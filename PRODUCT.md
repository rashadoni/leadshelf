# Product

## Register

product

## Users

LeadShelf is used by category managers, merchandising leads, store operations managers, and store employees in pharmacy and FMCG retail networks. Office users work on desktop screens to maintain product data, equipment, floor plans, planograms, assignments, reviews, and performance analytics. Store employees use the mobile flow to see execution tasks, follow the published planogram, submit photo proof, and leave concise feedback.

## Product Purpose

LeadShelf digitizes the merchandising workflow from catalog data to store execution. The product exists to make shelf planning operationally reliable: import and clean product data, model store equipment, build and publish immutable planogram versions, send execution tasks to stores, verify work with photo proof, and analyze shelf performance against sales and stock data. Success means a demo tenant can show the full loop without external data: catalog, equipment, floor plan, planogram, task, mobile proof, review, and analytics.

## Brand Personality

Operational, precise, and confident. The interface should feel like a serious planning and execution tool for retail teams: calm enough for repeated daily work, explicit about status and data quality, and direct in its language.

## Anti-references

Do not make LeadShelf look like a generic CRM, marketing dashboard, AI copilot, or decorative 3D demo. Avoid oversized hero sections, vague SaaS copy, unrelated CRM modules, decorative gradients, and workflows that hide the physical shelf model behind images only. Do not rely on AI/computer vision as the core product promise before the manual planogram, task, proof, and analytics loop is complete.

## Design Principles

1. Make the operating loop visible: every primary screen should connect catalog data, equipment, planogram, task, proof, and analytics.
2. Prefer clear operational density over marketing polish: users need fast scanning, comparison, and action.
3. Keep physical truth in the model: dimensions, shelves, placements, versions, and assignments must remain explicit.
4. Separate draft from published work: stores and tasks must reference immutable published versions.
5. Show data quality honestly: missing dimensions, empty proof, zero sales, and import errors should be visible and actionable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Default to WCAG AA contrast, visible focus states, keyboard-reachable navigation, readable table layouts at desktop and tablet widths, and reduced-motion-safe interactions. Mobile screens should prioritize task completion and proof submission over dense administration.
